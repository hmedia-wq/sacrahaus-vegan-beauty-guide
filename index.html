How to compare vegan skincare formats

A cleanser, toner, serum, cream, and oil have different jobs in a routine. Compare their ingredients and directions at the product level before deciding which format fits.

Start with the step you actually need

A cleanser is designed to be rinsed away. A toner, serum, cream, or oil usually stays on the skin, but the directions on the individual bottle decide how to use it. If you already have a cleanser you like, adding a second cleanser may be less useful than identifying a missing step. If your routine feels crowded, begin by comparing one format at a time.

Read beyond the front label

“Vegan” describes the absence of animal-derived ingredients; it does not tell you whether a formula suits your skin or whether every ingredient is acceptable to you. Read the full ingredient list if you avoid a particular fragrance component, botanical, or other ingredient. A product name can identify its format without explaining its concentration or how it feels when applied.

Compare products within the same step before comparing across steps. For example, the collection currently lists a Gentle Balancing Toner and a Witch Hazel Pore Perfecting Toner. Their names identify both as toners, but they are separate formulas. Check the respective product pages for the actual ingredients and directions instead of treating the names as interchangeable.

Keep face oils and creams distinct

The collection also lists a Gua Sha Face Oil, a Nourishing Jojoba Face Oil, and a Peptide Eye Gel-Cream. An oil and a gel-cream differ in texture and intended placement, so choose according to the directions for the specific item. An eye product is not automatically a substitute for a whole-face moisturizer. Likewise, a face oil's name does not establish that it replaces every other routine step.

A simple comparison: Write down the step, ingredient list, application directions, size, and price for two products you are considering. Then check whether either duplicates something you already use.

Check the live listing before buying

Collection pages can change as products and options change. Open the individual listing to confirm the current description, available variants, and price. Patch test a new topical product according to its label, especially if your skin is reactive. If you have a persistent skin condition or are unsure about an ingredient, seek advice from a qualified clinician rather than relying on a product name or broad beauty claim.
